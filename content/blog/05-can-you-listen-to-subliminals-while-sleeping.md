Yes, you can play audio while you sleep. The better question is whether it *does* anything beyond helping you relax, and how to set it up so it helps rather than disturbs.

## Why do people listen at bedtime?

The pre-sleep window feels like a natural moment for a ritual. The day is done, your phone is down, and a familiar voice and gentle sound can signal that it is time to rest. For many people, that signal is the main benefit.

## Does the brain learn while we sleep?

The research is more limited than the internet suggests. Early studies found that spoken sentences presented during verified sleep were not remembered afterward. More recent work shows the sleeping brain can form *simple* associations, such as pairing a tone with a smell, but that is a long way from absorbing a list of affirmations.

:::evidence
Sleeping brains can respond to sound and form some basic associations. There is no solid evidence that they absorb spoken self-help messages in a way that changes beliefs or behavior.
:::

## So what is the real value?

The part that is much better supported is **the wind-down itself**:

- A predictable routine helps many people fall asleep more easily [[VERIFY: cite sleep-hygiene guidance, e.g. a national sleep-medicine or health-service source]]
- Calm sound can mask distracting noise
- Slow, kind words can interrupt a spiral of worries

:::experience
Many listeners say they fall asleep faster with a consistent audio ritual. That fits with how routines work, whatever the audio contains.
:::

## How do I set up a restful session?

1. **Choose the right length.** Twenty to sixty minutes with a gentle fade is enough for most people.
2. **Keep the volume low.** You should barely need to focus to hear it.
3. **Use a soothing backing.** Rain, a soft hum or a steady tone, nothing sharp or sudden.
4. **Keep the lines short and calm.** "I let the day end" works better than a stream of goals.
5. **Plan the shutdown.** Use a timer or a track that fades, so you are not woken by the end.

## What about speakers versus headphones?

A small speaker on a nightstand is usually more comfortable than anything in your ears. If you use headphones, pick a soft sleep-friendly style, and avoid anything that digs into your head when you lie on your side.

## When should I skip it?

- If the audio keeps you awake or wakes you in the night
- If you rely on hearing your surroundings for safety
- If it makes you anxious. Sleep should feel safe

Persistent trouble sleeping deserves a conversation with a doctor. Audio is a gentle aid, not a treatment for insomnia or any sleep disorder.

## What does a good bedtime routine look like?

:::try
Dim the lights, put your phone on do-not-disturb, play a 20-minute session of three or four calm lines, and let it run out. Do the same thing tomorrow night.
:::
