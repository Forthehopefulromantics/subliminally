These two words come from the same root and mean nearly opposite things. Here is the quick version, then the details.

## What is the one-sentence difference?

- **Subliminal:** presented *below* the threshold of awareness. You do not consciously notice it.
- **Supraliminal:** presented *above* the threshold. You can consciously perceive it.

## What is the "threshold"?

It is the point at which you become aware of a stimulus. A whisper too quiet to hear, an image flashed too briefly to see, a sound masked by louder music: all fall below it. Researchers define and measure this threshold carefully, because deciding what counts as "unaware" is surprisingly tricky.

:::example
A billboard you read at a glance is supraliminal. A single frame hidden in a video, too quick to notice, would be subliminal.
:::

## Where does each show up in everyday life?

**Supraliminal** is the ordinary world: ads, slogans, signs, affirmations you read or hear clearly. You notice them and can think about them.

**Subliminal** is mostly a laboratory idea and a marketing label. Genuinely subliminal stimuli are used in experiments. In the wellness world, "subliminal" often describes audio where the voice is quiet or layered beneath music.

## Is most "subliminal" audio actually supraliminal?

Often, yes. If a recording is quiet but you could make out the words with attention, it is technically supraliminal. People still call it subliminal, and that is fine for everyday talk. It just helps to know that the label is looser than the lab definition.

## Which is more effective?

The evidence for hidden-message audio is weak. Research on subliminal self-help tapes has not found benefits from the hidden content itself. There is also no strong evidence that audible affirmations are *more* effective, but they have two practical advantages:

1. **You can check the wording.** You know exactly what you are rehearsing.
2. **You can consent to it.** You chose each line.

:::evidence
Studies of masked subliminal stimuli show small, short-lived effects in the lab. Studies of subliminal self-help audio have not shown benefits from hidden messages.
:::

## Which should I choose?

It depends on what you want from the practice.

- Want transparency and control? Choose audible affirmations in your own voice.
- Like a very subtle background feel? Lower the voice relative to the ambience, but keep it intelligible if you want to know what you are practicing.
- Want something meditative? A frequency or ambient layer with sparse, spoken lines works well.

:::experience
Many people find they like affirmations best when they can *just* make them out, as a soft presence rather than a hidden message.
:::

## Is there any risk in hidden messages?

The main concern is not mysterious influence. It is simply not knowing what you are listening to. Only use recordings from creators you trust, and prefer ones that tell you the content.

:::try
Listen to one of your own recordings with the voice slightly louder than usual. Notice whether being able to hear the words changes how it feels.
:::
